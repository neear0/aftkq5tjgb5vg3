What NAD+ is
  • A coenzyme present in every living cell. **It is not a peptide** — it belongs to the nucleotide cofactors.
  • It exists in an oxidised (NAD+) and a reduced (NADH) form; their ratio is an indicator of the metabolic state of the cell.
  • The literature describes cellular NAD+ levels falling with age.
Documented mechanism
  • It serves as an electron carrier in glycolysis, the Krebs cycle and the respiratory chain.
  • It is a substrate for the sirtuins (SIRT1–SIRT7), enzymes involved in the regulation of gene expression and in the response to metabolic stress.
  • It is also a substrate for the PARP enzymes in DNA repair and for CD38.
  • According to the literature, NAD+ availability therefore limits the activity of these pathways.
Areas of research
  • Mitochondrial function and energy metabolism.
  • Sirtuin activity and cellular ageing.
  • DNA repair.
  • The metabolic response to exertion and to energy restriction.
State of knowledge

The role of NAD+ in metabolism is settled biochemistry. The open question is

whether and how cellular NAD+ levels can be influenced from the outside — the

literature is divided on this, and most clinical data concern the precursors

(NR, NMN) rather than NAD+ itself.

Handling
  • The solution is sensitive to light and to repeated freezing.
  • NAD+ is hygroscopic — keep the vial open for as short a time as possible.
