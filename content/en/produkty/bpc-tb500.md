> A combined formulation of two reparative peptides in one vial.
> BPC-157 and TB-500, supplied as a lyophilised powder.

## What is in this combination
- **BPC-157** — a 15-amino-acid pentadecapeptide derived from a protein
  in gastric juice.
- **TB-500** — a synthetic fragment of thymosin beta-4, specifically the
  active sequence responsible for actin binding.
- The strength stated is the **combined mass of both substances** in the vial.
- The exact ratio is given in the certificate of analysis for the batch.

## Documented mechanism
- Published work on BPC-157 describes an effect on angiogenesis and VEGF
  expression in healing models.
- TB-500 is associated in the literature with the regulation of actin
  polymerisation and with cell migration.
- The combination is studied on the assumption that the mechanisms act at
  different levels of healing — cell migration versus vessel formation.
- A synergistic effect of the combination has not been demonstrated in
  controlled studies; it is an assumption derived from the mechanisms of the
  individual substances.

## Areas of research
- Healing of soft tissue, tendons and ligaments.
- Cell migration and proliferation.
- Remodelling of the extracellular matrix.

## State of knowledge
The data come from animal models, and separately for each substance.
Controlled studies of the combination itself in humans are not available.
The claim of synergy is a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Regulatory notice
Both TB-500 and its parent molecule thymosin beta-4 appear on the Prohibited
List of the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA). This item is a **reference
standard intended strictly for laboratory and research use**. It is not a
medicine and we provide no dosing or health advice with it.
