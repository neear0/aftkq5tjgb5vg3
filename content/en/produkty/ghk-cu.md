> A copper tripeptide — the glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine complex with a copper(II) ion.
> Three amino acids, supplied as a lyophilised powder.

## What GHK-Cu is
- A tripeptide of glycine, histidine and lysine, bound to a copper(II) ion.
- It occurs naturally in human plasma; the literature describes its level
  falling with age.
- The characteristic blue colour of the solution comes from the copper,
  not from impurities.
- It is among the best studied peptides in the dermatological literature.

## Documented mechanism
- Published work describes an effect on the expression of genes associated
  with collagen and elastin production.
- Studied as a copper carrier — copper is a cofactor of several enzymes in tissue.
- In models, an effect on remodelling of the extracellular matrix through
  metalloproteinases is described.
- Followed in connection with the antioxidant response.

## Areas of research
- Collagen production and skin structure.
- Wound healing and tissue remodelling.
- Hair follicle growth.
- Copper-dependent antioxidant mechanisms.

## State of knowledge
By the standards of research peptides, GHK-Cu has an unusually broad literature,
including in vitro data on human fibroblasts and genomic analyses. Controlled
clinical studies are largely in a cosmetic context and with topical application;
effects of other routes of use cannot be inferred from them.

## Handling
- The solution is light sensitive; keep it in the dark.
- The copper complex can react with some solvents — use bacteriostatic water,
  not a saline solution containing additives.
