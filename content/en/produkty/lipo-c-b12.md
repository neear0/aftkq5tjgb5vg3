> A combined formulation of lipotropic substances with vitamin B12.
> Supplied as a solution — unlike most of the catalog, it is not lyophilised.

## What is in this formulation
- **Lipotropic factors** — usually methionine, inositol and choline, referred to
  by the abbreviation MIC.
- **Cyanocobalamin or methylcobalamin** (vitamin B12).
- The exact composition and concentrations are given in the certificate of
  analysis for the batch; formulations differ between manufacturers.
- **It is not a peptide.** It is a mixture of an amino acid, B-group vitamins
  and vitamin B12.

## Documented mechanism
- Choline and methionine are described in the literature as methyl group donors
  in lipid metabolism.
- Inositol is part of the phosphatidylinositol signalling pathways.
- B12 is a cofactor of methionine synthase and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase — so it
  feeds into the same methylation cycle.
- The combination is built on the shared involvement of these substances in
  methylation metabolism, not on a demonstrated synergistic effect.

## Areas of research
- Lipid metabolism and fat transport in the liver.
- The methylation cycle and homocysteine.
- Nutritional deficiencies of B-group vitamins.

## State of knowledge
The individual components have well described biochemistry and, in the case of
B12 deficiency, clinical data as well. The combined formulation as a whole,
however, has no controlled studies, and claims about an effect on body
composition are not supported.

## Handling
- Supplied as a ready solution — it is not reconstituted.
- Light sensitive; B12 degrades on exposure to light. Keep in the dark and cold.
- Do not freeze.
