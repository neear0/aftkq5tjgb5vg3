> A small molecule — an inhibitor of the NNMT enzyme. It is not a peptide.
> Supplied as a lyophilised powder.

## What 5-Amino-1MQ is
- A small molecule of the quinolinium derivative class — **not a peptide**,
  unlike most of the catalog.
- Full name: 5-amino-1-methylquinolinium.
- A selective inhibitor of the enzyme nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT).

## Documented mechanism
- NNMT methylates nicotinamide, diverting it from the pathway leading to NAD+
  production.
- Published work describes that inhibiting NNMT increases the availability of
  nicotinamide in models, and with it cellular NAD+ levels.
- In models of obesity, increased NNMT expression in adipose tissue is described
  — this is where the interest in inhibiting it comes from.
- The mechanism is indirect: it does not raise NAD+ by supplying substrate, but
  by unblocking its consumption down another pathway.

## Areas of research
- NAD+ metabolism and its regulation.
- NNMT expression in adipose tissue.
- Energy metabolism of adipocytes.
- Skeletal muscle and regenerative capacity in models of ageing.

## Related items
Studied in the context of the same pathway as NAD+ and MOTS-C, both in the catalog.

## State of knowledge
The data come almost entirely from in vitro models and animal studies, chiefly
mouse models of obesity. Controlled clinical studies in humans are not available.
This is an experimental substance at an early stage of research.
