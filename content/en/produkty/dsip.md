> Delta sleep-inducing peptide — a nonapeptide isolated from blood.
> Nine amino acids, supplied as a lyophilised powder.

## What DSIP is
- A nonapeptide of nine amino acids, sequence Trp-Ala-Gly-Gly-Asp-Ala-Ser-Gly-Glu.
- First isolated from the blood of rabbits in a sleep phase — hence the name.
- Despite the name, its role in the regulation of sleep is disputed in the
  literature.

## Documented mechanism
- Published work describes an effect on delta waves in the EEG in some animal
  models; other work has not confirmed the effect.
- Studied in connection with the regulation of corticotropin and somatotropin
  secretion.
- An interaction with the stress response and with the hypothalamic-pituitary
  axis is described.
- No receptor has been identified — that is one of the main open questions.

## Areas of research
- Sleep regulation and EEG patterns.
- The neuroendocrine response to stress.
- Circadian rhythmicity.

## State of knowledge
DSIP is one of those peptides where the original findings from the 1970s and
1980s could not be replicated consistently. No receptor has been identified, the
mechanism is not elucidated, and controlled clinical studies to current standards
do not exist. The name of the peptide is historical, not a description of a
demonstrated effect — and it should not be read as a claim about sleep.
