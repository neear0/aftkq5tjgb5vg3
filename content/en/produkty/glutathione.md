> The tripeptide glutamate-cysteine-glycine, the main intracellular antioxidant.
> Three amino acids, supplied as a lyophilised powder.

## What glutathione is
- A tripeptide of glutamic acid, cysteine and glycine.
- Present in every human cell; in the cytosol it is the most abundant antioxidant.
- It exists in a reduced (GSH) and an oxidised (GSSG) form. Their ratio is a
  commonly used indicator of oxidative stress.
- The cysteine at the centre of the molecule carries a thiol group — that is
  where the antioxidant function comes from.

## Documented mechanism
- It neutralises reactive oxygen species directly through the thiol group.
- It is a cofactor of the glutathione peroxidases and glutathione S-transferases.
- It takes part in detoxification reactions in phase two of biotransformation.
- It contributes to regenerating vitamins C and E into their active form.

## Areas of research
- Oxidative stress and redox balance.
- Detoxification pathways and xenobiotic metabolism.
- Cellular ageing.
- Immune cell function.

## State of knowledge
The biochemical role of glutathione is well documented. What is disputed is the
availability of the administered molecule — the literature describes glutathione
being broken down into its constituent amino acids in the digestive tract, and
the efficiency of different routes of administration is a matter of debate.

## Handling
- The reduced form is sensitive to atmospheric oxygen — it oxidises after
  reconstitution.
- Keep in the dark and cold; prepare the solution as shortly before use as possible.
